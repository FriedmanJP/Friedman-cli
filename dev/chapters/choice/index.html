Discrete Choice, Count & Selection

Binary, ordered, multinomial, and count outcomes sit under estimate choice alongside full fitted-probability/marginal-effect prediction (predict choice) and residual extraction (residuals choice), while censored (tobit), truncated (truncreg), sample-selected (heckman), and stepwise (select) regressions sit under estimate regression. All discrete-choice, count, marginal-effect, odds-ratio, and selection leaves report 90% confidence intervals by default (-L/--conf-level 0.90). See MacroEconometricModels.jl — Discrete Choice & Selection for full likelihood derivations.

Quick Start

Simulate a binary cross-sectional dataset (logit), fit a binary logit model with HC1 standard errors, and compute average marginal effects and classification accuracy:

<!– capture –>

frdc --quiet data simulate cross-section --kind logit --n 200 --seed 11 --save-data bin_sim
frdc predict choice logit bin_sim --dep y --cov-type hc1 --marginal-effects --classification-table

Binary & Ordinal Models (logit, probit, ologit, oprobit)

Binary response models parameterize choice probability $P(y_i = 1 \mid x_i) = F(x_i'\beta)$ via logistic ($F = \Lambda$) or normal ($F = \Phi$) cumulative links, whereas ordinal models partition a latent index $y_i^* = x_i'\beta + u_i$ across ordered cutpoints $\alpha_1 < \dots < \alpha_{J-1}$:

\[P(y_i = j \mid x_i) = F(\alpha_j - x_i'\beta) - F(\alpha_{j-1} - x_i'\beta), \qquad j \in \{1, \dots, J\}.\]

  • Binary (logit, probit): Supports ols, hc0–hc3, and cluster standard errors (--clusters); predict choice computes average marginal effects (--marginal-effects) and confusion tables (--classification-table at --threshold 0.5).
  • Ordinal (ologit, oprobit): Outputs ordered_*_coefficients, estimated cutpoints, and fit_statistics. Under ologit, verify the proportional-odds / parallel-regressions restriction across outcome thresholds with test brant, and request generalized residuals via --generalized on residuals choice ologit.

Multinomial Choice & Count Models (mlogit, poisson, nbreg)

When categories are unordered ($j \in \{1, \dots, J\}$) or outcomes are non-negative counts ($y_i \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$):

\[P(y_i = j \mid x_i) = \frac{\exp(x_i'\beta_j)}{1 + \sum_{m=2}^J \exp(x_i'\beta_m)}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid x_i] = \exp(x_i'\beta + \ln E_i).\]

  • Multinomial Logit (estimate choice mlogit): Estimates alternative-varying slope vectors relative to the base category (multinomial_logit_coefficients); test independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) via test hausman-iia (--omit-category).
  • Rational-Inattention Logit (estimate choice ri-logit, predict choice ri-logit): Estimates Matějka–McKay (2015) endogenous-consideration discrete choice models across observed or --unobserved-state choice environments (--env-col, --choice-col, --alt-col, --attr-cols, optional [ri.logit] config); see Rational Inattention for the structural RI family (ri discrete, ri iia-test).
  • Count Regression (poisson, nbreg): Support optional --offset or --exposure controls and exponentiated incidence rate ratios (--irr). Test equidispersion ($\mathrm{Var}(y \mid x) = \mathbb{E}[y \mid x]$) via Cameron–Trivedi test dispersion before choosing Poisson over Negative Binomial (overdispersion_parameter).

Censoring, Truncation, Selection & Stepwise (tobit, truncreg, heckman, select)

When sample observability depends on outcome thresholds or selection rules, linear OLS is attenuated and biased:

  • Censored & Truncated MLE (tobit, truncreg): Handle lower and/or upper limits (--lower 0.0, --upper Inf) differently depending on whether limit observations remain in the sample (Tobit) or are omitted entirely (truncated).
  • Heckman Selection (heckman): Models outcome --dep with --outcome-vars and selection indicator --select with --select-vars via two-step inverse Mills ratio adjustment or full-information MLE (--method twostep|mle), testing correlation $\rho = 0$ via $\lambda$ in heckman_diagnostics.
  • Automated Covariate Selection (select): Runs forward, backward, bidirectional, best-subset, or Hendry–Doornik gets general-to-specific search (--keep forces required controls).

Common Pitfalls

  1. Ignoring perfect separation in small binary samples: When a binary predictor perfectly separates y = 0 from y = 1, logit/probit MLE coefficients diverge (model/convergence, exit 5); inspect cross-tabulations or rescale covariates.
  2. Omitting --select, --outcome-vars, or --select-vars in heckman: Without exclusion restrictions between --select-vars and --outcome-vars, two-step Heckman identification rests purely on nonlinearity of the inverse Mills ratio and exhibits severe collinearity.
  3. Confusing poisson --cov-type robust vs nbreg: Quasi-MLE Poisson with --cov-type robust yields valid standard errors under overdispersion whenever the conditional mean is correctly specified, whereas nbreg explicitly parameterizes conditional variance via quadratic dispersion alpha.
